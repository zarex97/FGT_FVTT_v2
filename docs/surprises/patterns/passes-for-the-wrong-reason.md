**Mechanism:** A test or probe stays green after the thing it guards stopped being true. Five files stubbed `Roll` for "every chance lands on 1", the test world began rolling Foundry's real dice over their stub, and they passed on the seed's luck. Three `check:world` probes expected "the model throws", and went on passing when the model threw for an unrelated reason (it could not load at all). The Dioscuri's drain-halving test handed the twin's distance in by hand, so it passed while the real board compiled the drain before the distance existed. A duration guard walked keys named `ticks` only, so it stayed green while the Circuits' `duration: "thisRound"` threw on first use.
**Lesson:** Assert the reason a guard fails (the message, the refused path), never only that something threw. When the test world takes over something a test used to stub, detect the stub and fail naming the new way (`w.dice`), rather than silently replacing it. A test that hands a function what its caller should supply proves nothing about the caller: every Glass Game test handed the script its history, and no boundary did (#247). When a guard's count falls after a rename, widen its pattern and never lower the count: the clock guard matched `combats?.active?.round` by spelling, and the move to `currentWar()?.round` read as six reads removed (2026-10-18).
