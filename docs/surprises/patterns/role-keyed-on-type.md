lesson: When code asks about a role (Master, summoner, owner, guard), ask about the relationship that confers it, the contract, never the actor's type, its side, the player who holds it, or what it carries. A role's fields are declared on every document type that can hold it. A role ends with its contract: ask whether it holds when asked.

**Mechanism:** A role is asked as a document type. "Is this a Master?" was `type === "master"`, so a Servant holding a Contract — Medea after Rule Breaker — was nobody's Master to the Contract, the multi-Servant tax and the freeing on defeat. The type check is right for every Unit the code was written against and silently wrong for the first that fills the role another way. Anastasia (#258) added Medea holding a Contract by Rule Breaker: a Servant could be a Master (`commandSpellsPerServant`) and had no `servantIds`, so its side of the Contract was silently dropped.
**Lesson:** Ask the relationship that confers the role: a Master is whoever holds a Contract (`masterId` points at it), a summoner is whoever `summonerId` names. When a new Unit fills an old role, grep every reader of that role for a type test. A side or match membership is the same mistake: a Master's guards and its Command Spells are its contracted Servants, not its faction's (#204). A Servant is what its contract names, not an actor: a legion of a hundred bodies is one Servant, so a grant per Servant is one per legion (#226). Never what an actor carries either: a role is held in this war, on this board (#230's legion offer asked every actor in the world). Nor the player: seven Masters one player held each offered heals on an attack none of their Servants was in; a card's offers are its Process's Masters' (#241). A role's fields are declared on every document type that can hold it. A role ends with its contract: ask whether it holds when asked.
